Pick a different content type per draft when the source allows; prefer the brand's own list. Templates:
- contrast-news: [Entity] did [A] while [B]; the gap is the story.
- tracker: where a topic stands now: a dated status, the number that moved, what is next.
- deadline-watch: [Entity or rule] takes effect on [date]; who must act and what to do first.
- explainer: one term or rule in plain words, one worked example, one takeaway.
- tax-consequence: an event ([action]) and what it means for the reader's tax position, with the rule's source.
- myth-vs-fact: the common belief, the actual rule, the source.
- enforcement-case: who was targeted, for what, what it shows other readers.
- brand-move: what the brand shipped or published, and why a reader should care.

## News
For requests that research recent news. One draft is one story. Choose and shape stories like this.

Story types, strongest first:
1. Competitive shift or duel under a regulation: a player loses a licence, is delisted or leaves while a competitor is authorised in the same period. A named loser and a named winner, and the reader has customers or money on one of them.
2. Pivot or before/after: the same entity changes sides (a crypto firm moves into AI or traditional finance, or the reverse; a security or infrastructure firm is bought by a financial group).
3. A large institution enters the field (a broker, bank, card network or central bank). Weaker than the first two: pick it only when the figures are large and the name is widely known.
4. A market move with a clear cause and a verifiable figure: only with a real contrast (bad news and a rising price) and a figure on a fixed date. Never state a cause the sources do not state.
5. A regulatory deadline that requires action (a consultation, a reporting date, an entry into force). The post must go out before the date.

Leave out:
- stories without a large, recognisable entity at the centre;
- vague plans without a date ("by 2027", "intends to");
- stories resting on a single tweet or a single secondary source;
- subjects already posted, and two stories in a row with the same central entity;
- political stories where the business fact cannot be separated from the commentary.

Audit: score each story from 1 to 10 on the criteria below and keep only 9 or 10. A few strong stories beat filler; when fewer qualify than requested, write fewer drafts and say why in `notes`.
- Entity clarity: the subject is clear at once, with no prior knowledge.
- Contrast: a real tension between expectation and reality.
- Figures: at least three verifiable facts, each with a source.
- Quotable line: one sentence someone would repost on its own.
- Fixed date: the event has a precise, recent date.
- Audience fit: it matters to this brand's audience, not merely interesting.
- Duel or pivot: it can be told as two entities face to face or as before and after.
- Taggable entities (LinkedIn): at least three companies with a page.

Hooks: two short sentences, each with a subject and a verb; the second turns the first. The entity is the first word; use short, physical verbs; no negation in key lines; no unexplained acronym. A report based on anonymous sources is attributed in the hook ("The [outlet] says ...").
- Duel: "[A] is out of [market]. [B] just moved in."
- Before/after: "[A] did [old thing] for years. Now it [new thing]."
Alternate the two across drafts.

LinkedIn news post, in this order: hook; context (one or two sentences with an absolute date and the key figure); a list title ending in a colon; three points, each with a figure or a name; one quotable line; either a relevance line addressed to the reader or a real question, never both; the brand line; a short CTA. No links in the body.
